# Proposal outline

Adapt the depth to the user's decision and available brief. A complete mixed-method proposal normally covers:

1. Decision summary and scope assumptions.
2. Current evidence, business context and unresolved questions.
3. Business-decision matrix and hypotheses with counterevidence.
4. Study phases, dependencies and qualitative-to-quantitative handoff.
5. Desk/internal research sources and expected outputs.
6. Qualitative method, recruitment matrix, sample rationale, interview modules, stimuli and analysis.
7. Quantitative target population, sample sources, base/boost design, quotas and comparison feasibility.
8. Quantitative module outline, recall periods, indicators, routing and interview burden.
9. Experiment or specialist module where the decision requires it; otherwise omit.
10. Coding, data quality, weighting, analysis and cross-market comparability.
11. Integrated interpretation, decision conditions and follow-on validation.
12. Deliverables, timeline, responsibilities, cost quantities, risks and open decisions.
13. Traceable sources and the boundary between historical reference and current fact.

Use tables for sample allocation, question-to-method mapping, timing and deliverables. Use prose for rationale and interview probes. Keep all proposed numbers labeled as design parameters. A plan can be internally reviewable while sourcing or budget remains open; it cannot be described as ready for fieldwork until its relevant dependencies are resolved.
