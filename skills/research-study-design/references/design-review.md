# Design and review a study

## Link the stages

For each decision, name the evidence that could change it. Use qualitative work to understand language, occasions, mechanisms and exceptions. Convert findings into constructs, response options, stimuli and rival hypotheses; preserve observations that contradict the preferred explanation. Quantitative work estimates patterns within its defined population/sample and tests specified comparisons. A joint display should show qualitative evidence, the quantitative measure, agreement/conflict and the resulting decision. Resolve disagreement by checking population, occasion, measurement and field conditions; do not average unlike evidence.

## Qualitative design

Choose interviews for personal routines and individual decision histories, groups for social interaction or co-creation, and observation/shop-alongs for enacted behavior. Select only what the problem needs. Describe recruitment states that can be applied without overlap, balancing attributes across the sample instead of crossing every attribute into tiny cells. Explain why cities and languages are included and what they exclude. Interview counts are a reasoned starting design; monitor unresolved themes and contradictory cases rather than promising saturation from a number.

Give module objectives, open starting questions, neutral probes, activities and output. Ask about a recent concrete occasion before opinions or projective tasks. Record unprompted responses before showing brands/concepts. Keep observed behavior, respondent explanation and researcher interpretation separate. Concept stimulus length, visual quality and information need comparable treatment. Account for consent, translation, recording and access in the actual work plan. Do not make participants represent an entire country.

## Quantitative design

Define who can enter, how they are reached and which decisions each analysis base supports. A category-buyer sample does not measure category incidence among all adults. Brand-customer boosts improve experience analysis but cannot be naively pooled into brand penetration or natural segment sizes. Retain source/sample-layer identifiers. Population calibration requires suitable external targets and assumptions; demographic weighting alone does not remove opt-in selection bias. State trimming, effective base and sensitivity choices when actual data are available.

Base sample size on important country/segment/experimental comparisons and plausible incidence, not a copied industry default. Cost and fieldwork assumptions remain assumptions until tested. For experiments specify assignment unit, allocation, primary outcome, manipulation, sample exclusions and comparison family before launch. Distinguish stated intention from observed purchase, and message/offer response from real-world incremental revenue. Low base, unstable segmentation or non-equivalent measures require narrower conclusions or additional evidence.

Outline the questionnaire in measurement order: unprompted before prompted, baseline measures before potentially contaminating stimulus exposure, overall experience before diagnostic attributes where that ordering matches the design. Bind definitions to the relevant respondent base and recall period. Put complex modules in separate versions/splits when interview burden requires it. Cognitive testing checks comprehension; a soft launch checks programmed routing, incidence, duration, quota feasibility and data quality. Material changes can make pilot interviews ineligible for pooling.

## Analysis and execution

An analysis plan names data, method, deliverable, decision and limitations. Advanced methods require an actual analyst/runtime and testable input assumptions; an outline is not a tested estimator. For segmentation, test alternatives, stability, assignability and business actionability. If no credible solution emerges, deliver needs/occasion profiles rather than forcing named segments. For pricing, distinguish reported paid price, stated acceptable price, controlled price response and estimated demand; choose the method accordingly.

Map deliverables to stage gates: brief/scope, qualitative design, qualitative-to-quantitative synthesis, questionnaire/stimuli/programming, pilot, clean data, analysis and recommendations. Maintain issue IDs and version dependencies. Show unit quantities for costing, with supplier prices and incidence still unconfirmed where appropriate. State fieldwork responsibilities and dependencies without inventing staffing or network access.

## Review questions

- Can every commercial question be answered by the planned evidence, and does each module have a decision use?
- Are markets, sample totals, subsets, boosts and experimental allocations internally consistent?
- Are methodological hypotheses distinguishable from source-backed facts and completed findings?
- Do time and cost plans include recruitment, translation, cognitive testing, soft launch, rework and review?
- Can a reader tell what can be reviewed now and what still needs confirmation before execution?
