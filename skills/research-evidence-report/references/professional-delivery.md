# A complete professional desk-research report

If desk research is part of the requested research package, deliver an actual evidence-backed report, not only a study plan, source list or empty template.

Inspect the reference's market definition, evidence, competition, consumer/occasion analysis, recommendations and source pages, including representative rendered slides. Learn the depth and decision structure without copying client conclusions, branding, signatures or unverified figures.

Cover the dimensions relevant to this study: scope and period, market signals and counterevidence, comparable competitor cases, local channel and price conditions, consumer-source sampling, opportunity judgments and handoff to qualitative/quantitative work. Do not manufacture market sizes, segment shares or scores to fill chapters.

- Keep year/fiscal year, geography, currencies, stock/growth, revenue/GMV and denominators explicit. Overlapping markets cannot be added into a TAM.
- Company growth does not establish causal impact. A successful licensed property does not prove every related category is growing; retain counterevidence.
- Separate buyers, recipients, households and transactions. Planned spending or stated interest is not observed purchasing or this project's fieldwork.
- Bind factual pages to sources and locators. A website feature proves a public feature, not coverage, conversion or good customer experience. Search snippets are discovery, not verified originals.
- Keep titles within the evidence's strength. Label inference and recommendations; link hypotheses to interview prompts, questionnaire IDs, experiment outcomes and possible disconfirmation.
- Deliver editable text, native tables/charts, visible citations and source notes. Keep full source snapshots local; publish only authorized original analysis, locators and necessary short excerpts.

Review substantive coverage, support, calculations, counterevidence, actionability, layout and Office validity separately. Open XML validation and SkillOpt replay scores do not certify research quality or institutional equivalence.
